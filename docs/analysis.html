Methodology & Metrics

Disclaimer: Model Limits

This tool is visualizes redistricting and its potential impact, and is not a live election forecast. Platforms like DDHQ, Cook Political Report, and VoteHub produce dynamic forecasts that incorporate daily polling averages, candidate fundraising ratios, advertising expenditures, and campaign developments. In contrast, this simple model takes into account only static electoral fundamentals by simulating baseline performance under uniform national swings. The charts should nonetheless reflect reality, with the exception of FL-22, which our model underestimates the flip chance of due to our limitations.

Chart 1: Redistricting Effectiveness on Individual Districts

Calculates whether redrawn district lines actually hold up under midterm wave conditions versus a neutral national baseline.

1. Partisan Baseline & Electoral Elasticity:
District baseline lean is adjusted by state-level swing elasticity (β_env) and incumbent status under national partisan swings:

μ = Partisan_Index + (β_env × National_Environment) + Incumbency_Offset

where National_Environment is expected to be 8.0 for the 2026 midterms due to aggregate polling, and 0 in a neutral environment.

2. Flip Probability:
Seat retention and flip odds are modeled using a logistic curve calibrated to historical House midterm variance (σ = 5.5):

P(Dem Win) = 1 / (1 + exp(-μ / 5.5))

3. Effectiveness Score:
The effectiveness score is taken as the average of the flip probabilities in both environments:

Effectiveness Score = ((P_flip_2026 + P_flip_neutral) / 2) × 100

A score of 100% indicates a statistical guarantee to flip in either environment, while a low score indicates an ineffective gerrymander where the targeted seat is favored to hold for the incumbent party.

Chart 2: State Delegation Expected Seat Shifts

Aggregates district-level probabilities to display the state's expected seat shifts due to redistricting.

1. Expected Net Gain (EV):
Sum of independent flip probabilities across all redrawn seats targeted by a state party:

EV = Σ P_flip(District_i)

2. Target Seats vs. Realized Value:
Compares a Party's total targeted flips (X-axis) against the realistic number of seats that would flip under each environment:

  • Full Realization (EV ≈ Target): Redistrict efforts successfully flip all intended seats. Only California is expected to do so for 2026. In a neutral environment, one of California's targets is not a lock, and Texas and Florida are still unlikely to reach their targets.
  • Dummymander: The case in which the gerrymandering Party ends up losing districts due to diluting their own districts too much. The only plausible application of this scenario this cycle is TX-34, which was slightly diluted (shifted 0.75% towards Harris).

3. Non-Standard General Elections:
For uncontested races or CA top-two intra-party runoffs (D vs D or R vs R), 2024 presidential results serve as the baseline partisan floor to prevent distortion.

Sidebar Metrics

Summary of definitions for the default overview and dynamic district-level metrics.

Mean Partisan Shift

The magnitude of partisan shift towards the gerrymandering Party, (mean(|Shift|)). For example, Louisiana's 6th district shifted from Harris+15.0 to Trump+31.8, a magnitude shift of 41.8 towards the right.

Median Partisan Lean

The median partisan lean across all targeted districts. The average new district targeted to flip is voted for either Trump or Harris by 11.6%.

Net Partisan Shift

The net partisan shift across all targeted districts, (Σ Shift / N). Because there's far more GOP-led gerrymanders than Dem-led ones, the average targeted district shifted 11.7% towards Trump.

Median District

The middle seat of targeted districts when sorting the new districts by their post-redistricting 2024 presidential margin.

Tipping Point Seat

The seat that determines control of the House: the 218th seat when ranking all 435 national districts from most Democratic to most Republican. Due to gerrymandering this cycle, the GOP would have gained an additional 1.9% popular vote advantage for House control under the 2024 environment.

Generic Ballot Advantage

Measures structural seat bias: (Seats Won / 435) - National Popular Vote Share. On average, the GOP maintains a 2.79% popular vote advantage for the 2026 midterm House elections.
